## 3. What the review changes in the recommendation

INV-W linear mean stands as the base, with three changes:
1. Proxy mean as a generalized-least-squares mean with the empirical residual correlation (or ISC mb and NEIC mb treated as one measurement), so that σ is honest for the Tinti–Mulargia weights.
2. One Mw scale for direct values: W family first (NEIC Mww, GUC Mwr, CSN web Mw, NEIC Mwr −0.10, GFZ −0.03, CSN Mww last below 5.0), GCMT shifted onto that scale below 5.4. Equivalent alternative if DECISIONS keeps GCMT as the nominal reference: map the W family onto GCMT with a magnitude-dependent offset. The numbers barely change; what matters is that it's one scale.
3. A period split at 1990 as a CV candidate for ISC mb and NEIC mb, kept only if the CV and the rate check support it.

Stated assumptions for the paper: selection acts on the reference, not on the native scale (true for mb and ML, checked in C); σy of 0.08–0.10, with λ treated as a ±0.05 systematic at native 4.5.

## 4. Rate correction: the sign depends on the estimator (new, affects the DECISIONS line on Tinti–Mulargia)

CEUS-SSC (EPRI/DOE/NRC 2012, NUREG-2115, §3.3.1, read in ch. 3):
- A magnitude measured with error (M̂) over-counts. True rate = N(M̂)·exp(−β²σ²/2), and E[M] = M̂ − βσ² (Eq. 3.3.1-5).
- A regression estimate E[M|X] under-counts. True rate = N(E[M])·exp(+β²σ²/2) (Eq. 3.3.1-11).
- They adopt N* = exp(β²σ²/2) per event, counted at E[M] (Eq. 3.3.1-12).

Our inverse conversions are M̂-type: they're unbiased for M given x, like a direct measurement. So "Tinti–Mulargia weights as the USGS N*" can't be applied literally. Either weight the events by exp(−β²σ²/2), or first shift each to E[M] = M̂ − βσ² and then use N*. Musson (2012) warns that the wrong choice makes the bias worse. With σ ≈ 0.22 and β ≈ 2.3 the two directions differ by about ±13 % in rate, so the proxy σ has to be honest as well (§2, row 7).

## 6. Draft methods paragraph

> Native magnitudes were converted to Mw with one linear relation per agency and scale. The relations were fitted on Chilean event pairs by regressing the native magnitude on the reference Mw and inverting, with the slope corrected for the reference error. Global moment-tensor catalogues are incomplete below Mw 5.2–5.6 (period dependent), so the usual regression of Mw on the native magnitude selects pairs on its dependent variable. That is a truncated regression, and it is biased at the low end (Hausman and Wise 1977; Lolli et al. 2014, 2023). Regressing on the truncated variable removes the bias, provided the native scale is reported independently of size, which holds for mb and ML (Supplement). Below Mw 5.4 the reference is the regional and W-phase moment-magnitude family, whose members agree within 0.03 after constant offsets. GCMT reads 0.04–0.10 higher there, as found by Gasperini et al. (2012), and above 5.4 the two are identical (Lolli et al. 2023). Direct Mw takes precedence over proxies; several proxies are combined by a generalized least-squares mean. The homogenized catalogue keeps b within 0.13 for minimum magnitudes 4.7–5.8 and its M ≥ 5.5 rate within ±10 % across network periods, and a Gutenberg–Richter fit on Mw 5.0–6.5 predicts the observed number of M ≥ 7 events (40.5 against 41). Forward conversions predict 12 % of them.
