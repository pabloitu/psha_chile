## Magnitudes (A5)
- Catalog cut at Mw 4.5 for the spatial pattern; conversions must be accurate at 4.5–6 (10 Nov). Fits start at Mw 5.0 and completeness is read with the events from Mw 3.5 (15 Nov, see Statistics).
- Conversions fitted on Chilean pairs only (matched by time and distance, no magnitude criterion) by inverse regression: the native magnitude is regressed on Mw, the slope corrected for the Mw error, and the relation inverted. Forward fits (Mw on native) are biased high below native ~5.5 because GCMT is incomplete there (X1, X2). The inverse step is new for this purpose; it is argued in the paper from truncated regression (Hausman and Wise 1977; Lolli et al. 2014, 2023). Published relations are a check only (11 Nov).
- Forms per scale: a straight line, or a line with a saturation hinge; split by depth (50 km) or period (1990, ISC and NEIC mb) as candidates; chosen by time-block cross-validation with the one-standard-error rule (the simplest form wins unless another is clearly better). Outside the data range the straight part continues and σ grows (11 Nov).
- One Mw scale (Hanks and Kanamori 1979 constant). Above Mw 5.4 GCMT first. Below 5.4 the W-phase and regional Mw (NEIC Mww, GUC Mwr and CSN web Mw, NEIC Mwr + 0.08, GFZ Mw + 0.04, CSN Mww last below 5.0; offsets measured on shared events) come before GCMT, following Lolli et al. (2023); a GCMT value below 5.4 that stands alone is lowered by 0.162 × (5.4 − GCMT), a line fitted on 812 pairs that is zero at 5.4, so there is no step (0.10 at 4.75, 0.06 at 5.0). Lolli et al. (2023) leave such values unchanged; we keep the line, as the magnitude-dependent offset of GCMT to the W-phase family, the same principle as the constant offsets of the other members (13 Nov).
- A CSN regional Mw more than 0.3 from the median of two or more other moment magnitudes of the event is left out (quality check, 26 values) (13 Nov).
- One Mw per event: Ruiz and Madariaga (the events it names), then a direct Mw in the order above, else one proxy by a fixed priority list of scales ordered by measured scatter (as Allen et al. 2018; Christophersen et al. 2024): NEIC and ISC surface-wave magnitudes, CSN ML, ISC mb, NEIC mb, INPRES ML. Converted magnitudes come only from the agencies that observe Chile directly or are the global references (ISC, NEIC, CSN / GUC, INPRES / SJA); every other agency of the ISC Bulletin and the coda, duration and untyped magnitudes are not converted, and an event with nothing else has no Mw and is listed. Surface-wave magnitudes are used without a magnitude limit, only for events not deeper than 60 km (Di Giacomo et al. 2015; Engdahl and Villaseñor 2002; Weatherill et al. 2016); a limit at Mw 5 biases the count at Mw ≥ 5 by +5 to +10 % or −7 to −15 % (X4). One converted scale per event; the weighted mean of the scales is a sensitivity case because it lowers large events where mb saturates. Potin magnitudes are not used (11–12 Nov). Before 1976 (13 Nov, after A6): a moment-based magnitude goes before a converted one, and inside each level the Centennial goes before ISC-GEM (S. Ruiz): Ruiz and Madariaga > Centennial Mw (Pacheco and Sykes, Harvard) > ISC-GEM direct Mw (flag d) > Centennial Ms, mB and untyped values converted by their type > ISC-GEM proxy Mw (flag p) > Cabello (before 1904). From 1976 a measured Mw (GCMT, W-phase and regional family) goes before Ruiz and Madariaga, whose value is used where no measured Mw exists and whose class is always used. The CSN moment magnitude listed as GUC MW in the ISC Bulletin (noisier than the others, sd 0.20, and reaching Mw 2) is not used to fit the relations or offsets; as an event Mw it comes last among the measured moment magnitudes, with the 0.3 cross-check (13 Nov, after run 2026-11-13d). Conversion relations are fitted only on pairs with a reference Mw ≥ 4.0 (Pablo, 13 Nov): the catalogue starts at 4.5 and smaller events must not steer the fit; a floor on the reference does not bias a native-on-Mw regression. Run 2026-11-13e: the floor moves no relation by more than 0.01 at 4.5–5.5. Each event keeps its σ; values dithered within the native 0.1 bin (11 Nov).
- Acceptance tests of the homogenized catalogue: b range ≤ 0.2 over fit floors 4.7–5.8; M ≥ 5.5 rate steady across 1976–89 / 1990–99 / 2000–10 / 2011–26; a GR fitted on Mw 5.0–6.5 predicts the observed M ≥ 7 count within the Poisson band, overall and per family; converted values within 0.1 of independent regional Mw; no comb or heap at 4.5–5.5 (11 Nov).
- Rate bias from magnitude uncertainty: every event counts exp(−β²σ²/2) with its own σ and the β of the fit (CEUS-SSC 2012, eq. 3.3.1-1 applied per event); no magnitude is moved; the same rule for all events, no logic-tree branch, no cap on σ (Pablo, 15 Nov). It lowers the rates by 8–11 % and leaves b as it is. Support: on the same events the converted magnitudes give about 8 % more events at Mw ≥ 5.6–6.0 than the measured ones, as expected from their errors; on synthetic catalogues the uncorrected N(≥ 5) is 10 % high (B9).
- σ of the historical values of Cabello et al. stays 0.5 for all (Pablo, 15 Nov, after B11). Their source (CERESIS-GEM) lists 0.2 for 27 of the 43 events of Mw ≥ 7 and 0.6 for 15; with those values N(≥ 7) of the interface is 3 % and N(≥ 8) 4 % higher. The ISC-GEM values of quality D (all from its Supplement, σ 0.7–1.1, 23 events of Mw ≥ 5.0, largest 6.6) stay as they are: no effect on the fits.

## Classification
- Depth kinds: measured, assigned, unknown. Where an event has no measured depth but the literature states its class (Ruiz and Madariaga 2018, or a source accepted in A6), the depth is assigned from that class and Slab2 at the epicentre: interface = the slab top, kept within the 10–50 km of the interface model; in-slab = inside the slab, by the convention of the in-slab sources; crustal = none, unless the literature gives one. Assigned depths are flagged, carry a large σ, and are never used to classify or to test a rule. The class comes first and the depth follows; no other depth is filled from the slab model (13 Nov).
- An event located below the slab counts as in-slab (general rule, covers Potin's too-deep depths under the flat slab; in-slab source depths come from Slab2 anyway) (11 Nov).
- Rules rebuilt in A7 (`tasks/A7_classification.md`, 13 Nov), few and general, no patch rules for single events:
  - Each event carries a weight per class (interface, in-slab, crustal, outer rise) adding up to 1; the rates sum the weights. A class from the literature (Ruiz and Madariaga 2018, A6 hints) has weight 1 and goes first.
  - Over the shallow slab (top ≤ 50 km) the depth gives class scores after Haynie et al. (2026): interface within ± 15 km of Slab2, fading between 50 and 65 km depth; crustal and in-slab share the rest (Moho fixed at 40 km). The scores are averaged over the depth error of the event (a normal distribution cut at the ground; ours, not in Haynie et al.) (14 Nov, run 2026-11-14a, X5).
  - Interface mechanism: the Slab2 plane slipping in the plate convergence direction (one azimuth, 257°). Misfit m = √((swing/20°)² + (twist/45°)²), smallest of the four descriptions of the double couple. m ≤ 1: interface odds × 3.9; m > 1: no interface. A mechanism never decides alone. Chosen over the Kagan angle and over "a thrust on the contact is interface" by six tests (literature events, mechanisms withheld, Riedel-Hornig et al. 2026 classes and depths, synthetic catalogues) (14 Nov, X5).
  - A weight ≥ 0.8 becomes 1, otherwise the event is split (kept 14 Nov). Deeper slab: depth with its error above or below the slab surface. With our depth errors no rule classes more than about two thirds of the events over the shallow slab correctly (synthetic test, X5); the split weights carry that uncertainty.
  - No depth: outer rise west of the trench; crustal where there is no slab; in-slab where the slab is deeper than 50 km and all rejected depths are ≥ 70 km; otherwise split by the shares of the classified events in the same position and magnitude bin. Fits run with and without the split events.
  - No depth, before 1900, in the forearc, no hint: no crustal share, whatever the slab depth; the share goes to interface and in-slab in proportion. Under the arc and backarc the shares stay; their large events have A6 hints. Reason: a forearc crustal event large enough for the historical record is rare and its damage local, while the shares of 0.06–0.13 of great interface events made most of the forearc's observed Mw ≥ 7 (Pablo, A9, 16 Nov).
  - The arc class ends where the arc polygon (`data/intraarc.shp`) ends, 47.24° S, for events and faults (`classify.arc_end()`); 46.15° S (`SOUTH_LAT`) only chooses the trench (Nazca or Antarctic) (Pablo, A9, 16 Nov).
  - Events within 75 km east of the trench between 45.5° S and 46.15° S are outer rise: Chile Ridge fracture-zone events at the triple junction (strike-slip and normal mechanisms), left out of the source model (Pablo, A9, 16 Nov).
  - Sub-classes by position only. Class names: interface, antarctic_interface, intraslab_shallow, intraslab_intermediate, jujuy_nest, crustal forearc / arc / backarc / Patagonia, outer rise.
- Double seismic zone: not a catalogue class; the two planes are handled in the in-slab source model (plan C) (13 Nov).
- In-slab classes: shallow (slab top above 50 km at the epicentre), intermediate, and the Jujuy nest as a class of its own, so that it does not contaminate the in-slab model: within 0.8° of 66.9° W, 24.0° S, 150–320 km, measured depth. The upper limit was moved from 170 to 150 km on the depth histogram of the cluster (Pablo, 14 Nov; 1 054 events of Mw ≥ 4.5).
- Antarctic interface kept as a class for completeness; its source uses the geodetic model only, not the catalogue rates (13 Nov).

## Statistics (B9)
- Completeness is a property of the catalogue, not of the tectonic class: it is read on all events, by region and period, and one table is used for every class. A larger magnitude is never complete later than a smaller one (Pablo, 15 Nov).
- The table is automatic. From 1964 the steps come from the shape of the magnitude distribution with the events from Mw 3.5: Mc by b-stability and a Kolmogorov–Smirnov test per network window and region, the largest value, and a magnitude is complete from the window after the last one whose Mc is above it. Before 1964 a step is the earliest year from which the counts of a 0.5 band stay consistent with the instrumental Gutenberg–Richter line (5-year blocks, BIC), on all events (15 Nov).
- Completeness table, every class: Mw 5.0 from 1976, 5.2 from 1964, 7.0 from 1905, 7.5 from 1830, 8.0 from 1500 (15 Nov). Mw 4.5–4.9 is complete over the whole margin in no period and is not used in the fits.
- Rules that compare rates between periods (Stepp, change points, a joint likelihood) are not used from 1964 on: the sequences of 2010–2015 set the level and earlier periods read as incomplete (B9).
- Estimator: maximum likelihood on the table with the class weights, event bootstrap (2 000 resamples); the bins above the Mmax of the unit are left out of the likelihood. Kijko and Smit (2012) is a check of the table (same b within the bootstrap error on a table that fits) and b-positive a check of b (15 Nov).
- The fitted line is never open. Two shapes are fitted for every unit, each by its own likelihood, and both go to the tornado: tapered (Kagan 2002a; preferred) and cut at Mmax (Weichert 1980). The tapered line does not end at Mmax (Pablo, 15 Nov).
- One rule for Mmax and corner in every unit, interface included (Pablo, 15 Nov): Mmax = largest observed event of the unit + 0.2; the cut shape ends at Mmax; the corner of the tapered shape is computed from that Mmax and the b of the unit by Kagan (2002b, eq. 12), so that the tapered line releases the moment rate of the cut line (corner about 0.21 below Mmax for b 1.15, about 0.27 for b 0.96). The evenly discretised tapered list ends at Mmax + 0.2 and its last bin carries the rate of the tapered line above it, so N(≥ M) equals the fitted line at every edge; with the Kagan corner 0.6 % of the moment lies above Mmax + 0.2 in every unit (Pablo, 15 Nov, S1).
- Rates and b from the full catalogue in every class: completeness table, fits, checks and bootstrap use all events, not declustered (15 Nov; as In-slab and Crustal below, now also the interface fits of B11).
- A historical step of the automatic table is kept only if it reaches at least 10 years before 1964 (15 Nov, B11; replaces dropping the steps of 1955–1960 by hand).
- Fits on the A9 catalogue: `fits/tables/fits_2026-11-16.csv` (one row per unit and shape; one Mmax rule and the Kagan corner; completeness table `table_2026-11-16.csv`, the same steps as on 15 Nov), report `fits/FITS.md` section 8 (16 Nov). It replaces `fits_2026-11-15b.csv` (refit of B12 step 0) and `fits_2026-11-15.csv` (first B11 table), both kept. a in the convention of the shape: N(≥ M) = 10^(a − bM) − 10^(a − b Mmax) for the cut line, 10^(a − bM) times the taper for the tapered line.
- Declustering method is one tornado factor with two options, applied jointly to the declustered interface rate branch and to the in-slab and crustal patterns: Gardner–Knopoff (van Stiphout et al. 2012 windows, foreshock window = aftershock window) and nearest neighbour (Zaliapin and Ben-Zion 2013; b 1, d_f 1.6, epicentral distance, one mixture threshold from 1964, the largest event of a cluster kept). Both run per class family, Mw ≥ 4.5, the whole span (`catalog/declustered/gk_family_2026-11-15.csv`, `nn_family_2026-11-15.csv`); weights 0.5 / 0.5 until the tornado (Pablo, 15 Nov, B12).
- Declustered fits use the B11 code, the completeness table and the Mmax of the full catalogue (`fits/tables/fits_declustered_2026-11-16.csv` on the declustered catalogues `catalog/declustered/*_2026-11-16.csv`, methods gk_family and nn_family, table full; report `fits/DECLUSTER.md` section 6; replaces the 2026-11-15 files, kept). The declustered tables move the historical steps up to 80 years earlier because the rate rule is one-sided; reported, not used (Pablo, 15 Nov, B12).
- Patagonia crustal: b of the forearc, own rate. Backarc and Jujuy nest: own fit at the floor of the table, no pooling; rough estimates (Pablo, 15 Nov).
- Interface b is a tornado factor with three options: b per segment, one b shared by the four segments (pooled, family b 0.958; removes the failure at 32–26° S) and the fit of the whole margin (Pablo, 15 Nov, after B11).
- Source MFDs go to OpenQuake as evenly discretized lists: bin rates are differences of the fitted cumulative line at the bin edges (`gr.rates` in `fits/gr.py`), `min_mag` at the first bin centre, the tapered list ending at Mmax + 0.2 with the rate of the line above in its last bin. Each shape has its own a; a and b are never re-entered into an OpenQuake formula of another shape (15 Nov, B11).
- The Ogata–Katsura joint fit is not used: it does not work on this catalogue (B11). No sensitivity fit with later historical completeness steps (Pablo, 15 Nov); instead the paper states that the automatic rule does not recover a known table on synthetic catalogues (historical steps 30–75 years early, N(≥ 7) 6–14 % low).
- Left as they are and named in the discussion of the paper (Pablo, 15 Nov): the intermediate in-slab events of Mw ≥ 7 before 1976, which are more than the fit expects (29 against 18 on this table, by weight; old magnitudes probably too high), and the step Mw 8.0 from 1500, where the magnitudes before about 1730 are the least certain (the rule accepts it on 15 observed against 17 fitted). Also left open: the shortfall of intermediate in-slab events at Mw 5.0–6.4 in 1964–1975 (B11).

## Interface
- Rates: seismic branch and geodetic branch (moment balance), weights 0.5 / 0.5; no moment-deficit approach (Sep–6 Oct).
- Geodetic: convergence 6.6 cm/yr fixed, coupling χ 0.7 / 0.8 / 0.9 (0.125 / 0.25 / 0.125 of the total weight) (6 Oct).
- Geometry: full margin and four segments, 0.5 / 0.5 (Sep).
- MFD: tapered GR (preferred) and cut at Mmax, both into the tornado, by the one rule of Statistics: Mmax = segment maximum + 0.2, corner from Kagan (2002b) (Pablo, 15 Nov; replaces the fixed corner 9.6 of Bird and Kagan 2004).
- Mmax: largest historical event inside each segment + 0.2 (9.7 / 9.3 / 8.7 / 9.0; 9.7 for the whole margin), the same rule as every other unit (Pablo, 15 Nov; replaces "no pad", 6 Oct); moment closure only as a diagnostic. It bounds the likelihood of both shapes and ends the cut line.
- Z_TOP 10 km, Z_BOTTOM 50 km fixed, stated (6 Oct).
- Rupture scaling: Strasser 2010, Thingbaijam 2017 and Allen and Hayes 2017 (bilinear), 1/3 each, as a setMSRAbsolute level of the logic tree applied to every interface source, the Antarctic one included; aspect ratio 1, rake 90° (Pablo, 15 Nov, S1).
- Interface sources (S1, 15 Nov; `model/interface/REPORT_S1.md`): complex faults on the Slab2 top 10–50 km, one NRML file per branch (`model/interface/nrml/branches.json`), sources from Mw 5.5 (bin centre), integration distance 600 km on the rupture distance. Reference tree: tapered shape and the b of each unit; the cut line and the shared and margin b options are written with weight 0 for the tornado ("margin b": segment rates refitted with the b of the margin fit). The Antarctic source takes the b of the 45.6–37° S fit of the same branch.

## In-slab
- In-slab depth model (C14, 15 Nov; run 2026-11-15e, `model/inslab/REPORT_C14.md`; rerun 2026-11-16 on the A9 catalogue, identical cells, `depth_cells_2026-11-16.csv` is the file to read): one point source per cell; seismogenic zone from the Slab2 top to 0.475 of the Slab2 thickness below it (the fraction that holds 95 % of the in-slab events; the same in the catalogue and in Riedel-Hornig, at every latitude 16–36° S and every magnitude). Inside it three equal slices give the hypocentre distribution: weights the share of the events in each slice, smoothed with the kernel of the rate pattern (plus one event per slice), hypocentres the median in each slice. One layer: the median of the zone. Ruptures are not bound to a slice; OpenQuake centres each rupture on its hypocentre and keeps it inside the zone, so from about Mw 7.3–7.5 a rupture fills the zone.
- The map pattern stays 2-D and uses all in-slab events (split weights and unknown depths included); only the depth weights use depths. Depths of the depth model: the final catalogue from 1990, class weight 1, without ISC, CSN web and ComCat depths (long tails below the slab), and Riedel-Hornig et al. (2026) inside Chile (Ml ≥ 2.5, depth error ≤ 5 km), for depths only. Local-network depths east of the Chile border are left out: they are 10–50 km deeper than ISC-EHB L1 there at 22–34° S. Jujuy nest left out. One layer against three is a tornado factor (13 Nov; depth sources Pablo, 15 Nov).
- Upper and lower seismogenic depth of each in-slab point source: the zone above (slab top to top + 0.475 × thickness), so large ruptures fill it whatever slice their hypocentre is in (checked in OpenQuake 3.26, C14).
- Riedel-Hornig et al. (2026) (automatic, relocated, 2017–2021, 18–43° S, 620 000 events, depth error about 6 km; Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.15284376): depths for the in-slab depth model only; not a location source of the catalogue (C14 benchmark: inside Chile Potin and ISC-EHB L1 agree with it within 2–4 km; it is too deep east of the border, as its authors say, p. 12–13).
- Rates and b from the full catalog; declustered catalog only for the spatial pattern (Marzocchi and Taroni 2014) (Sep–24 Oct).
- No zonation; equal pattern weights (30 Sep). Kernel: Helmstetter et al. (2007), adaptive power law (exponent 1.5, bandwidth the distance to the n-th neighbour, minimum 5 km). n is calibrated per class, in-slab and crustal alike, on one train/test split: declustered events before 2010 against 2010 to today, scored by the CSEP S-test log-likelihood, with candidates 5–100. The final pattern uses all data up to today; a class with fewer than 20 test events takes its family's n (Pablo, 15 Nov; replaces the fixed 25 neighbours for in-slab and 15 for crustal).
- In-slab kernel n (S2, 16 Nov, A9 catalogue): the log-likelihood is scored at n 1, 3, 5, 10, 15, 25, 50, 100 (kept for analysis); n is the first local maximum from n = 5 up, and a second maximum at large n is ignored. Without the floor the intermediate class would take n = 1, which is unreasonable (Pablo, 16 Nov). Result: 5 for every class and both declustering options, except 10 for the shallow class under nearest neighbour (the same as 15 Nov) (`model/inslab/tables/calibration_2026-11-16.csv`, figure `figs/2026-11-16/kernel_loglik.png`).
- In-slab pattern events enter as rates: class weight over the completeness period of their magnitude, w / (t_end − T_c(m)), with T_c(m) from the completeness table; in the calibration the training events take w / (2010 − T_c(m)), and the test events keep w because they share one period. No magnitude weighting and no fixed start year (Pablo, 16 Nov, S2).
- Two nodal planes: N–S strike, 60° dipping west normal and 30° dipping east reverse (30 Sep).
- Mmax: observed maximum + 0.2 per class (Sep); historical events count (Pablo, 15 Nov). On the frozen catalogue the observed maximum is 8.0 in both classes: Santiago 1647 (shallow; without it 7.8, 1918) and Calama 1950 (intermediate; Chillán 1939 is 7.8). Tapered shape: corner from Kagan on Mmax (Statistics).
